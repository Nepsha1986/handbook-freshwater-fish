## Overview

The Variatus Platy (_Xiphophorus variatus_) is a close relative of the [platy](/en/xiphophorus-maculatus) and the
[swordtail](/en/xiphophorus-hellerii), with one important trait that often goes unnoticed: it is the most cold-tolerant
species in the genus. In the wild it lives in the rivers and streams of north-eastern Mexico, from Tamaulipas to
Veracruz, where winter temperatures drop well below what the tropics deliver. Hence its reputation as a fish for the
unheated aquarium.

The variatus is slimmer and more elongated than the common platy, and males carry a tall, often raised dorsal fin.
Adults reach 4-6 cm, with females larger and fuller than males. They live 3-5 years, are active by day and rest among
plants at night.

Colour is the main reason for the species' popularity. The wild form is olive-yellow with a few dark vertical bars, but
selective breeding has produced many varieties: sunset (a red-to-yellow gradient), tiger, greenish-yellow, black-red and
"parrot". A hallmark of many strains is the contrast between a yellow or orange tail and a darker body.

Like the rest of the genus, the variatus hybridises readily with the platy and the swordtail. Keep the species on its
own if you want a pure line; in a community tank the offspring will almost certainly be hybrids.

## Tank Requirements

The recommended minimum is 80 litres for a group of six to eight fish. That is more than the common platy needs, and for
good reason: variatus platies are noticeably more active, cruising the tank all day and needing open swimming space. A
tank 60 cm or longer beats a tall one.

Optimal water parameters:

- **Temperature:** 20-26°C. This is the key point: the variatus thrives at a room temperature of 20-22°C and needs no
  heater in a heated home. Keeping it permanently at 26°C or above shortens its life.
- **pH:** 7.0-8.0. Neutral to slightly alkaline.
- **Hardness (GH):** 10-30. Soft water does not suit this fish; below GH 8, add crushed coral to the filter.

The layout is the standard livebearer setup: dark substrate, thickets of vallisneria, cryptocoryne, hornwort and elodea
along the back and sides, an open centre. The cool water variatus platies prefer suits most undemanding plants nicely.
Floating plants shade the tank and shelter the fry.

A medium-output filter is enough, and the fish appreciate a gentle current. Always cover the tank: variatus platies are
lively and jump more readily than common platies.

One practical note: it is precisely this cold tolerance that makes the variatus a good candidate for outdoor ponds and
garden containers during the warm season. Above 18°C they live outside happily all summer, breed prolifically, and come
back indoors before the first cold snap.

## Feeding and Diet

Variatus platies are omnivores, and plant matter matters to them as much as to any other poeciliid. In the wild they
graze algae and pick small invertebrates off the surface.

Base the diet on quality flakes or livebearer pellets containing spirulina and other plant ingredients. Offer live or
frozen food — brine shrimp, daphnia, cyclops, small bloodworms — two or three times a week, and blanched vegetables such
as courgette, spinach or lettuce once a week.

Feed once or twice a day in small portions eaten within a couple of minutes. In cool water the fish's metabolism slows,
so reduce portions accordingly: overfeeding in cold water digests poorly and leads to gut problems more often.

Variatus platies graze hair algae and soft biofilm enthusiastically, helping keep the tank clean.

## Care and Maintenance

Care comes down to routine.

Change 20-25% of the water weekly, matching temperature and hardness. Vacuum the substrate every one to two weeks. Rinse
filter sponges only in tank water you have just siphoned out.

Check ammonia, nitrite and nitrate every one to two weeks: the first two at zero, nitrate below 40 mg/l. Variatus
platies are tough, but as organic waste builds up their colour is the first thing to go — a faded group almost always
means water changes were skipped.

Keep a group of at least six with females in the majority: two or three per male. The males are peaceful but persistent
courtiers, and without enough females one or two will be worn out.

The health problem you are most likely to meet is ich, usually following a sudden chill. Gradual seasonal temperature
shifts bother these fish not at all, but a drop of five or six degrees at once is another matter.

## Compatibility

The variatus is peaceful and leaves its neighbours alone. Choose tank mates by temperature first: not every tropical
fish is willing to live at 20-22°C.

Good tank mates:

- [white cloud mountain minnow](/en/tanichthys-albonubes) — a perfect match in temperature range;
- [zebra danio](/en/danio-rerio) — equally comfortable in cool water;
- [platy](/en/xiphophorus-maculatus) and [swordtail](/en/xiphophorus-hellerii) — with the hybridisation caveat;
- [molly](/en/poecilia-sphenops) and [guppy](/en/poecilia-reticulata) — if you run the upper end of the temperature
  range;
- [bronze corydoras](/en/corydoras-aeneus) and [bristlenose pleco](/en/ancistrus-cirrhosus) — calm bottom dwellers.

Poor choices: heat-loving species that need 26-28°C — there is no way to combine them with a variatus without one side
losing out. Also avoid the [tiger barb](/en/puntius-tetrazona) and other nippers, along with large cichlids, to which a
variatus is simply food.

## Breeding

Variatus platies are livebearers, and reproduction in a community tank takes care of itself — no encouragement needed.

The female carries her brood for 28-35 days and gives birth to 20-60 fully formed fry that swim and feed immediately. In
cool water gestation stretches out, which is perfectly normal. An imminent birth shows in an angular belly and a
darkened gravid spot near the anal fin. The female stores sperm and can deliver several more broods with no male
present.

To save fry, dense moss and floating plants are usually enough: 10-20% survive in a well-planted tank. If you want the
whole brood, move the female to a planted 10-20 litre container and return her right after birth. Use breeding traps
only briefly — confinement can trigger premature delivery.

Feed the fry four to five times a day on crushed flakes, baby brine shrimp and microworms. They grow faster at 24°C than
at 20°C, so it is worth running the grow-out tank at the upper end of the range. Sex is distinguishable at two to three
months and maturity arrives at three to five, though full colour develops much later, closer to a year old.
